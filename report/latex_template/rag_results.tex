\subsection{RAG Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:rag_results}

\paragraph{Retrieval baseline.}
On 125 development queries, exact dense
retrieval achieved Recall@5 of 0.379,
Recall@10 of 0.475, Recall@20 of 0.580,
and Recall@100 of 0.724. MRR@10 was
0.460 and NDCG@10 was 0.398.
Recall increased substantially with
retrieval depth, but higher recall alone
did not imply better generated answers.

\paragraph{Generation quality and cost.}
Table~\ref{tab:rag_context} reports
automated answer quality for fixed
context sizes and the oracle condition.
The highest observed fixed-context mean
was 0.341 at $N=10$, but quality varied
only modestly from $N=3$ to $N=20$.
Increasing context from five to twenty
documents raised prompt-token usage by
approximately 4.1 times without a
corresponding improvement in judged
answer quality.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and interpretation.}
Relative to $N=5$, the paired quality
difference was $+0.011$ for $N=10$
(95\% CI $[-0.041,+0.062]$) and
$-0.011$ for $N=20$
($[-0.066,+0.043]$). Both intervals
include zero, so the experiment does
not establish a quality advantage for
larger fixed contexts. The oracle
condition scored 0.239 higher than
$N=5$ (95\% CI $[+0.173,+0.305]$),
indicating substantial headroom when
gold evidence is supplied directly.

\paragraph{Adaptive selection and latency.}
Similarity-gap thresholds of 0.015
and 0.025 selected means of 4.99 and
10.30 documents, respectively.
Their mean quality scores were 0.331
and 0.320, with no established
improvement over fixed $N=5$.
In a separate 20-query, single-worker
timing subset, mean generation latency
rose from 4.53\,s at $N=5$ to
6.08\,s at $N=20$. These results
illustrate the cost of larger contexts,
but the small timing subset limits
generalisation.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The evaluation relies on automated
judging and one generation per
query--condition pair. Judge variability
and the limited development split
make small score differences uncertain.
The oracle comparison diagnoses an
evidence-availability bottleneck;
it does not isolate retrieval as the
only source of answer errors.
