\subsection{RAG Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:rag_results}

\paragraph{Retrieval baseline.}
On 125 development queries, exact dense
retrieval achieved Recall@5 of 0.379,
Recall@10 of 0.475, Recall@20 of 0.580,
and Recall@100 of 0.724. MRR@10 was
0.460 and NDCG@10 was 0.398.
Recall increased substantially with
retrieval depth, but higher recall alone
did not imply better generated answers.

\paragraph{Generation quality and cost.}
Table~\ref{tab:rag_context} reports
quality and API-reported mean prompt tokens
for every condition. The highest fixed-context
mean was 0.341 at $N=10$. Increasing from
$N=5$ to $N=20$ raised mean prompt tokens
from 1,208 to 4,951 (4.1$\times$), without
a corresponding quality improvement.

\paragraph{Paired comparisons and stability.}
Relative to $N=5$, the paired differences were
$+0.011$ for $N=10$
(95\% CI $[-0.041,+0.062]$) and $-0.011$
for $N=20$ ($[-0.067,+0.042]$). The adaptive
policies also showed no established advantage:
gap 0.015, $\Delta=+0.001$
($[-0.052,+0.055]$); gap 0.025,
$\Delta=-0.010$ ($[-0.062,+0.041]$).
Oracle versus $N=10$ gave $\Delta=+0.229$
($[+0.167,+0.292]$); versus $N=5$,
$\Delta=+0.239$ ($[+0.175,+0.307]$).
The oracle uses at most 10 gold records, but
is not matched to fixed $N=10$ in document
count or token length.

On 40 matched $N=5$ repeat queries, the
mean repeat-minus-primary score difference
was 0.000 (paired-difference SD 0.218);
29/40 normalized combined scores were
identical. Because this repeat changes both
generation and judgement, it measures combined
score variability and cannot isolate either
source; it is not answer-text stability. The
20-query, single-worker timing subset measured
mean generation latency of 4.53\,s at $N=5$
and 6.08\,s at $N=20$.

\paragraph{Gold-evidence presence.}
Using saved \texttt{ctx\_recall} values
($>0$ versus $=0$) to stratify queries, $N=5$
scored 0.467 in 73 queries and 0.138 in 52;
$N=10$ scored 0.434 in 82 and 0.163 in 43.
This is observational, not causal.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Automated judging on a limited development
split leaves judge variability unquantified
and small score differences uncertain.
